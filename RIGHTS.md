## 5. Canon and Creative Freedom

Official character settings do not prohibit fan interpretation.

A derivative work may intentionally differ from official canon.

For example, creators may:

- depict characters in original situations;
- design original costumes;
- reinterpret visual styles;
- create alternate timelines or universes;
- alter characterization for parody or storytelling;
- create original 3D interpretations;
- create mods or game-specific adaptations;
- or otherwise transform MachiVerse material creatively.

Such works do not change the official MachiVerse canon merely by existing.

The authoritative MachiVerse canon remains whatever is designated as canonical by Kazuto Hashimoto or official MachiVerse documentation.

The relevant distinction is:

> **Changing something in your own derivative work is permitted.**

but:

> **Falsely claiming that your modification replaces the official canon, or gives you ownership or exclusive control of the underlying MachiVerse IP, is not permitted.**

Where reasonably necessary to avoid confusion, substantially non-canonical material should be presented as unofficial, derivative, alternate, fan-created, or otherwise non-canonical.

## 6. Forks, Derivative Projects, and Separately Branded Projects

Software forks remain governed by their applicable software licenses.

For Apache-2.0 licensed code, no additional permission from Kazuto Hashimoto is required merely to create or distribute a lawful software fork.

Creative assets are governed separately by this policy.

A substantial separately branded project that wishes to use MachiVerse-owned creative assets must contact Kazuto Hashimoto in advance.

For example:

If CityVerse also wishes to use MachiVerse characters, artwork, music, 3D models, branding, or other separately managed creative assets, prior contact and permission are required.

Reasonable requests will generally be **considered positively** unless there is a significant concern involving:

- deception;
- legal conflict;

- reputational harm;
- hostile appropriation;
- or similar unreasonable circumstances.

Conditions may be added concerning attribution, branding, redistribution, commercial use, or clarification that the derivative project is unofficial.

The purpose of this requirement is primarily **coordination, not prohibition**.

## 8. Original Asset Redistribution

Creative freedom does not grant an unrestricted right to republish official source assets as a reusable asset library.

Unless separately authorized, official source assets must not be redistributed primarily as standalone reusable materials.

Examples include:

- republishing official illustrations as an asset pack;
- creating a mirror whose main purpose is redistribution of official assets;
- redistributing official textures as a texture library;
- repackaging official 3D source models;
- redistributing voice or music files as a sound library;
- or offering substantially unchanged official source files for independent download.

This restriction does not prevent reasonable inclusion of assets within a permitted MachiVerse-related finished or derivative work.

For example, an asset may be included as a functional part of:

- a fan game;
- a mod;
- an animation;
- a video;
- a website;
- a derivative software project;
- a presentation;
- or another permitted work.

The relevant distinction is between:

> **using an asset as part of a work**

and:

> **redistributing the asset itself as the product.**

## 16. Repository Contributions

Software code and creative assets are intentionally governed differently.

For that reason, MachiVerse repositories should avoid relying solely on a single repository-wide contribution assumption when accepting creative assets.

### 16.1 Code contributions

Unless otherwise agreed:

> **Code contributions submitted to portions of a MachiVerse repository designated as Apache-2.0 licensed are submitted under the Apache License 2.0.**

Contributors must have the rights necessary to submit their contributions.

### 16.2 Creative asset contributions

Creative asset contributions must not be accepted casually into an Apache-2.0 software contribution workflow.

The preferred policy is:

> **Third-party creative asset contributions are not accepted through the ordinary Apache-2.0 code contribution process unless explicit asset-specific terms have been agreed.**

Where practical, third-party creative asset contributions should instead be handled through:

- a separate asset repository;
- a dedicated asset-contribution process;
- an explicit contributor agreement;
- or another clearly documented asset-specific arrangement.

Creative asset contributions require confirmation that:

1. the contributor has the necessary rights to submit the material;
2. the contributor understands the applicable asset terms;
3. the contribution is not unintentionally being submitted under Apache-2.0;
4. the rights granted to Kazuto Hashimoto and/or the MachiVerse Project are clearly documented.

### 16.3 Default repository practice

A public software repository may contain bundled or referenced creative assets, but this does not mean that open creative-asset submissions must be accepted there.

A recommended model is:

```text
MachiVerse/
├─ src/
│  └─ Apache-2.0 software
├─ assets/
│  └─ bundled or approved assets
└─ CONTRIBUTING.md
```

with creative asset contributions managed separately where appropriate.

For larger-scale asset collaboration, a separate repository such as:

```text
MachiVerse-Assets/
```

or an equivalent dedicated asset repository is preferred.

### 16.4 Contribution acknowledgement

If a repository accepts third-party creative assets, the contribution process should require explicit acknowledgement substantially equivalent to:

```text
[ ] I understand that this contribution contains creative assets.

[ ] I confirm that I have the rights necessary to submit these assets.

[ ] I understand and agree that these assets are governed by the
    specifically identified creative-asset terms or contributor agreement,
    rather than being treated as Apache-2.0 software contributions merely
    because they are submitted to a MachiVerse repository.
```

The applicable asset terms or agreement must be identified before acceptance.

### 16.5 Repository documentation

Repositories accepting external contributions should maintain a `CONTRIBUTING.md` or equivalent notice distinguishing:

- Apache-2.0 software contributions; and
- separately governed creative asset contributions.

An `ASSET_POLICY.md`, `RIGHTS.md`, directory-level README, or equivalent notice should identify the rights applicable to bundled creative assets.

## 20. Policy Updates and Existing Works

This policy may be updated from time to time.

Each published revision should identify its policy version and effective date.

Unless required by law or reasonably necessary to address:

- fraud;
- infringement;
- deception;
- abuse;
- hostile rights appropriation;
- serious safety concerns;
- or similarly significant circumstances,

changes to this policy are **not intended to retroactively invalidate works that were lawfully created in reasonable reliance on a previous version of this policy.**

### 20.1 Creation and private preparation

A creator may rely on the policy version that was effective when the relevant work was lawfully created for purposes of its creation and private preparation.

For example, a fan work lawfully created under Policy v1.0 does not become improperly created merely because Policy v2.0 later introduces different conditions.

If that work has not yet been publicly distributed when a new policy becomes effective, its first public distribution may be subject to the policy then in effect.

### 20.2 Works already publicly distributed

A version of a work that was lawfully published or distributed in reasonable reliance on an earlier version of this policy may, in principle, **continue to be distributed under the terms of that earlier policy.**

A later policy revision is not intended, by itself, to require compliant creators to withdraw an existing version of a work that had already been lawfully released.

This continuity applies to the existing version and substantially equivalent continued distribution of that version.

### 20.3 New or materially expanded activity

The then-current policy may apply to future activity such as:

- first public distribution of a previously private work;
- materially updated or substantially revised versions;
- new editions with material changes;
- new commercial exploitation;
- new merchandise;
- new sublicensing;
- new separately branded projects;
- new categories of distribution;
- or substantially expanded use.

Routine continued availability of an already lawfully distributed existing version is not, by itself, intended to be treated as a new use requiring compliance with every later policy revision.

### 20.4 Serious circumstances

Nothing in this Section is intended to prevent action reasonably necessary to address:

- fraud;
- infringement;
- deliberate deception;
- serious abuse;
- hostile rights appropriation;
- unlawful activity;
- or comparable serious circumstances.

Such exceptional action should be directed at the relevant conduct and should not ordinarily affect unrelated creators or works that remain compliant with the applicable policy.

### 20.5 Version records

Git history, release history, archived policy copies, or another dated publication record may be used to identify which policy version applied at a relevant time.

This Section is intended to provide reasonable continuity and confidence to community creators while allowing current rules to govern genuinely new or materially expanded future uses.
